### 1. `history_and_geography.txt`
> Chapainawabganj is a historical district located in the northwestern part of Bangladesh, bordered by India to the west and north. It is famous for its rich historical background dating back to the ancient Bengal Sultanate and the British colonial era. Key historical landmarks include the Choto Sona Masjid, Tahkhana Palace, and Darasbari Mosque located in the historic region of Gaur. The Mahananda River flows through the district, dividing the region and eventually meeting the Padma River, making the area geographically unique with fertile alluvial soil.

### 2. `mango_and_economy.txt`
> Chapainawabganj is widely known as the "Mango Capital of Bangladesh" due to its extensive production of high-quality mangoes. The district produces popular varieties such as Khirshapati (Himsagar), Langra, Gopalbhog, and Amrapali, supplying major domestic and international markets. The local economy heavily relies on agriculture, seasonal mango harvesting, sericulture (silk production), and traditional handicrafts. Agro-based industries and cold storage facilities play a crucial role in the district's economic growth and seasonal employment.

### 3. `culture_and_transport.txt`
> The culture of Chapainawabganj is deeply rooted in traditional Bengali heritage, influenced by local dialects, folk music like Alkap and Gombhira, and vibrant rural festivals. Gombhira is a traditional folk performance unique to this region, historically used to raise awareness on social issues with wit and humor. For transportation, the district is connected via road networks and river routes along the Padma and Mahananda rivers. Sonamasjid Land Port serves as a major international trade gateway between Bangladesh and India, boosting regional commerce.
