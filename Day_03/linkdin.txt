Today marked a major milestone in my AI and systems engineering journey. I built and successfully validated a complete, localized RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) Pipeline prototype from scratch.

Building robust search systems requires more than just calling an API—it demands precise architectural decisions around chunking, indexing, and context preservation. Here is a breakdown of what I implemented and learned today:

    Granularity Experimentation & Ingestion:
    Processed a multi-domain document corpus, testing three distinct architectural layers: Whole Document, Section, and Paragraph. Evaluated how each layer handles context and token overhead.

    Deterministic Stable ID Scheme:
    Designed and enforced an indexing contract utilizing MD5 hashing (source_uri + section_name + paragraph_index) to ensure chunk IDs remain fully immutable across re-indexing runs for reliable source citations.

    Parent-Child Metadata Linkage:
    Implemented a hierarchical linking mechanism where every small child chunk retains a parent_id pointing directly to its parent section, successfully solving context window fragmentation.

    Corpus Failure Mode Analysis:
    Documented real-world system bottlenecks observed during testing:

    Whole Document: Suffers from high token overhead and information dilution.

    Section: Occasionally groups disparate sub-topics, lowering search precision.

    Paragraph: Risks fragmenting critical context (mitigated by parent-child bridges).

Key Takeaway: Choosing the right retrieval unit and establishing a solid ID contract is the backbone of building production-grade, hallucination-free RAG systems.
