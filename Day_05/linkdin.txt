A lot of people think vector search and AI retrieval are pure magic, but today I got my hands dirty at the lowest level to see how Vector Embeddings and Semantic Search actually work under the hood.Here is a breakdown of what I learned, built, and tested today:How AI Understands Meaning: Computers don't read text—they process geometry. I explored how full sentences are mapped into 768-dimensional dense vector spaces where semantically similar ideas sit physically close to each other.Asymmetric Retrieval & Query Prefixes: Searching with a 3-word question against a 500-word passage usually breaks standard models. I tested BGE's asymmetric query prefixes and saw how explicitly instructing the query aligns short questions with long passage contexts.Matryoshka Embedding Magic: High-dimensional vectors eat up memory and drive up vector database bills. I tested vector truncation by cutting embeddings from 768 down to 256 dimensions ($L_2$ re-normalized)—and maintained 100% Recall@10 with 0% recall loss while slashing memory overhead by 66.6%!Overcoming Hardware Bottlenecks: Rather than choking my local machine with heavy PyTorch builds, I migrated the pipeline to Google Colab. Using BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5, I generated normalized matrices for 1,000 document chunks, ran cosine similarity benchmarking across 20 test pairs, and persisted the binary output matrices (.npy) and aligned IDs (.txt) for indexing.Seeing the math, code, and retrieval accuracy align in real time made all the theory finally click into place. Onward to building production-grade vector search and hybrid retrieval engines!
